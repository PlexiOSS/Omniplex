Your bot's page is where people decide whether to add it. A clear, honest page gets more invites and more votes than a long list of buzzwords.

## The short description

This is what people see on cards and in search, so lead with what the bot does. "Moderation, leveling and tickets with a web dashboard" tells people more than "The best all-in-one bot ever".

## The long description

Aim for something people can skim:

- **Start with the main features.** Two or three sentences on what makes your bot worth adding.
- **Show how to get started.** The first command to run, and how to configure it.
- **List the key commands**, or link to your full command list. You can also [document commands on Omniplex](/kb/listings/documenting-commands) so they show on the Commands tab and in the [Command Directory](/commands).
- **Say where to get help**: your support server or docs.

Markdown and HTML both work, including headings, lists, links, images and code blocks. Scripts and anything that could harm visitors are stripped out.

## Make it look the part

- **Add a banner** from your dashboard. See [Vanity URLs and Assets](/kb/listings/vanity-and-assets).
- **Pick accurate tags.** They decide where your bot shows up when people browse.
- **Add extra links** to your support server, website or source code.
- **Post updates** in the [changelog](/kb/listings/changelog-updates) so people can see the bot is maintained.

## Keep it honest

Don't copy another bot's description, promise features you don't have, or fill the page with keyword spam. The [Page Rules](/kb/bots/page-rules) apply to everything on it, and reviewers check them before approval and after.
